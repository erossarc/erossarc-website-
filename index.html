Blockchain & crypto

Crypto, explained without the hype.

The mechanics behind the coins — how the ledger works, how trading actually happens, and why the four-year cycle keeps showing up in every chart.

01

What is a blockchain?

A blockchain is a distributed ledger — a record of transactions copied across thousands of independent computers instead of sitting on one company's server. Every new block references the hash of the one before it, which is what makes the chain tamper-evident: change one entry and every block after it stops matching. Bitcoin secures this with Proof-of-Work; Ethereum has run on Proof-of-Stake since the 2022 Merge.

No single party controls the chain — consensus is maintained by nodes spread across the globe.
02

How crypto trading works

Crypto markets never close. Centralised exchanges like Binance and Coinbase match buyers and sellers directly, while decentralised exchanges such as Uniswap route trades through on-chain liquidity pools instead of an order book. Price moves on a mix of order-book depth, liquidity, large-wallet activity, and macro risk sentiment.

Core vocabulary: spot, futures, perpetual swaps, funding rate, open interest.
03

Bitcoin vs altcoins

Bitcoin behaves like digital gold: a hard-capped, store-of-value asset with no smart-contract layer by design. Everything else — Ethereum's programmable contracts, Solana's throughput, and thousands of smaller tokens — trades with a different risk profile, liquidity depth, and use case. Bitcoin dominance, its share of total crypto market cap, is one of the simplest sentiment gauges in the space.

04

Market cycles & halving

Roughly every four years, Bitcoin's block reward is cut in half — a hard-coded rule that reduces new supply entering the market. Historically, bull runs have followed six to eighteen months later, though correlation with Fed policy and broader risk appetite means the pattern is a lean, not a law.

The 2024 halving cut the block reward from 6.25 BTC to 3.125 BTC.
Foreign exchange

Forex runs on time zones, not tickers.

The forex market trades roughly 24 hours a day across four overlapping sessions, handing off from one financial centre to the next. Majors carry the tightest spreads and deepest liquidity; minors and exotics move more, and less predictably.

01

Sessions & liquidity

Sydney opens the trading day, handing off to Tokyo, then London, then New York. The London–New York overlap sees the heaviest volume of the day, which is when most major pairs make their biggest moves.

02

What actually moves a pair

Interest-rate differentials, central bank tone, inflation prints, and employment data are the usual drivers behind a currency pair's direction. A pair is a relative bet — EUR/USD rising can mean the Euro is strong, the Dollar is weak, or both at once.

TradingView guide

TradingView, the trader's cockpit.

The industry-standard charting platform used by retail and institutional traders alike. Here's the shortlist of what's actually worth learning first.

PS

Pine Script

TradingView's own scripting language. Custom indicators and full strategies, from a simple moving-average crossover to multi-condition entry logic, all run inside the chart.

CT

Chart types

Beyond candlesticks: Heikin Ashi smooths noise, Renko filters small moves by ignoring time, Point & Figure does the same in a different way, and Range bars normalise for volatility.

MTF

Multi-timeframe analysis

Professional traders align signals across timeframes — the higher one sets direction, the lower one refines the entry. Multi-pane layouts make this a one-screen exercise.

BT

Strategy backtesting

The built-in Strategy Tester runs Pine Script logic against historical data — net profit, max drawdown, Sharpe ratio and trade-by-trade results, before any capital is at risk.

AL

Alerts & webhooks

Price, indicator, or drawing-based alerts fire to email, SMS or push. Webhooks let those same signals reach an external bot or execution system.

Commodities

Gold & oil, the older markets.

XAU

XAU/USD — Gold

Gold is the classic safe-haven asset. Traders watch it for risk-off signals, inflation hedging and Dollar weakness — it tends to move inversely to real interest rates, rising as rates fall.

  • Trades nearly 24/7 via futures and CFDs
  • Driven by Fed policy and Dollar strength
  • Key support / resistance: $1,800 / $2,100
OIL

US Oil — Crude (WTI)

West Texas Intermediate is the benchmark for global oil pricing. It's driven by OPEC+ supply decisions, weekly US inventory data, geopolitical tension and global demand forecasts.

  • OPEC+ decisions can move price 3–8%
  • EIA inventory report every Wednesday, 15:30 GMT
  • High correlation with broader risk sentiment
